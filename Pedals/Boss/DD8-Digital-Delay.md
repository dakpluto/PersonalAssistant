# Boss DD-8 Digital Delay

Digital delay pedal offering 11 delay modes in one stompbox, from clean digital repeats to analog/tape emulations, pitch-shifted shimmer, glitch, and a built-in looper — successor in the long-running Boss DD delay line, now with true stereo I/O.

## Controls
- **MODE**: Selects the delay type. 11 modes: Analog (BBD-style warm repeats), Standard (clean digital delay), Tape (vintage tape-echo emulation), Warm (softer-sounding digital delay), Reverse (backwards-repeat effect), +RV (digital delay with reverb blended into the repeats), Shimmer (pitch-shifted, lush/ambient repeats), Mod (digital delay with modulation added to the repeats), Warp (expressive, pedal/expression-controlled delay for ambient swells), GLT (glitchy, stuttering/machine-gun repeat effect), Loop (looper with overdub, up to 40 seconds recording time).
- **TIME**: Delay time. Adjustable up to several seconds depending on mode; also settable via tap tempo (up to 10 seconds).
- **FEEDBACK**: Number of repeats — clockwise for more repeats/longer decay, counterclockwise for fewer.
- **E.LEVEL (Effect Level)**: Output level of the delay signal, mixed with the dry signal.

## Footswitches / Switches
- Single footswitch: engage/bypass, and doubles as tap-tempo entry (press and hold ~2 seconds to arm tap-tempo mode, then tap in rhythm).
- **Carryover switch**: when engaged, existing delay trails continue to ring out naturally after the pedal is bypassed, instead of being cut off abruptly.
- True stereo input/output, with selectable output routing (independent, panned, or wide stereo) for stereo rigs.
- TEMPO/EXP jack accepts an expression pedal or external footswitch (e.g. FS-5U, FS-6, FS-7) for tap tempo or real-time control of mode-dependent parameters (e.g. Warp mode).

## Notes for patch-building
- Belongs at the end of the drive/modulation chain, in the traditional time-based-effects position — after distortion/EQ, typically alongside or before reverb.
- The Loop mode makes this pedal double as a basic looper (up to 40 seconds with overdub) — worth noting separately from its role as a delay if a patch calls for looping.
- Carryover is the key control for patches that need delay trails to ring out through a bypass/section change rather than cutting off — turn it on for that behavior, off if a hard cutoff on bypass is wanted.
- True stereo I/O makes it a good candidate when a rig/patch has a stereo return path (e.g. into two amp inputs or a stereo effects loop); mono use is fully supported too via a single output.
