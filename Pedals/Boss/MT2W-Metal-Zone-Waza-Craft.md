# Boss MT-2W Metal Zone (Waza Craft)

High-gain distortion pedal, Waza Craft reissue of the original 1991 MT-2 Metal Zone — one of the best-selling distortion pedals ever made, known for scooped-mid, high-gain metal tones and an unusually powerful onboard EQ. The Waza Craft version adds a Standard/Custom mode toggle on top of the original circuit.

## Controls
- **DIST**: Distortion/gain amount. Low settings stay usable as a tight rock crunch; upper range gets very high-gain and compressed/fizzy on chords.
- **LEVEL**: Output volume.
- **LOW**: Bass EQ band, boost/cut.
- **HIGH**: Treble EQ band, boost/cut.
- **MID**: Midrange EQ band level, boost/cut — works together with MID FREQ.
- **MID FREQ**: Sweepable center frequency for the MID band (roughly 200 Hz–5 kHz), making the EQ semi-parametric. This is the control that gives the MT-2 its scooped-mid or nasal/honky voicings depending on where it's set.

## Footswitches / Switches
- Single footswitch: engage/bypass (buffered bypass — not true bypass).
- **Mode switch** (Waza Craft addition, front panel): Standard vs Custom.
  - **Standard**: reproduces the original MT-2's mid-focused sound, with improved clarity and lower noise floor than a vintage unit.
  - **Custom**: a wider-ranging, more dynamic high-gain voicing with a fuller, rounder low end than Standard mode — more of a modern high-gain amp-in-a-box feel.

## Notes for patch-building
- Place after any drive/boost stages and before the amp block — it's a full distortion voice on its own, not a transparent boost, so it typically substitutes for (rather than stacks cleanly with) an amp's own gain stage.
- The EQ is unusually powerful (up to 15 dB per band with a sweepable mid) — it can function as a tone-shaping/scoop stage as much as a distortion, so it's a reasonable stand-in wherever a patch wants a fixed, aggressive EQ curve baked into the drive itself rather than relying on a separate EQ module.
- Buffered bypass: safe for long cable runs and won't load down pedals earlier in the chain, but it's always somewhat in the signal path even when off (very minor, but relevant on boards mixing buffered and true-bypass gear).
- Custom mode's tighter low end suits drop-tuned/high-gain metal parts; Standard mode is the closer match to the classic '90s scooped-mid metal tone most people associate with the original MT-2.
