# Boss BF-3 Flanger

Compact flanger pedal covering classic jet-swoosh flanging through more extreme and rhythmic variations, including a high-gain "Ultra" mode and a gated/panning mode. Successor to Boss's BF-2/BF-1 flanger line with expanded mode options and tap tempo.

## Controls
- **RATE**: Speed of the flanging LFO sweep
- **DEPTH**: Intensity/width of the sweep
- **MANUAL**: Sets the base delay/center point the sweep moves around (interacts with mode — acts as resonance/manual sweep center depending on mode)
- **RES (Resonance)**: Amount of feedback in the flanging comb-filter, increasing the intensity/metallic character of the effect
- **MODE**: Selects the flanging character/behavior — standard flanger, an "Ultra" mode for a more extreme/intense sweep, and a "Gate/Pan" mode that gates and auto-pans the effect rather than sweeping continuously (also includes stereo and mono-slicer/gate-flanger variants depending on model generation of the switch)

## Footswitches / Switches
- Single pedal switch: standard mode toggles the effect on/off (flanging restarts from the low end each time it's engaged); a separate Momentary mode applies the flanging effect only while the switch is held down, cutting off the instant it's released (tap tempo is not available while Momentary mode is active)
- Tap tempo: tap the pedal switch in rhythm (outside Momentary mode) to sync the RATE to a desired tempo

## Notes for patch-building
- Standard modulation-pedal placement: after drive/distortion, before delay/reverb — flanging sounds cleanest driven by an already-shaped/distorted signal rather than in front of gain stages (where its comb-filtering interacts unpredictably with distortion clipping).
- Stereo output lets it spread the flanging sweep across two amps/channels for a wider effect where the rig supports stereo.
- Momentary mode is well suited to a single dramatic swoosh (song intro/transition hit) rather than a sustained flanging texture — use standard on/off mode for a flange that needs to ride through a section.
- Distinct from a chorus (like the CE-2W) in that it uses a shorter delay with resonant feedback, producing the characteristic metallic/jet-swoosh comb-filtering rather than chorus's thicker, non-resonant pitch-wobble doubling.
