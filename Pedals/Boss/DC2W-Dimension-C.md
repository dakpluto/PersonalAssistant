# Boss DC-2W Dimension C (Waza Craft)

Waza Craft-series reissue/expansion of the classic Boss DC-2 Dimension C, itself a compact recreation of the legendary rack-mount Roland SDD-320 Dimension D studio chorus/spatial processor. Instead of rate/depth knobs, it uses four preset mode buttons (which can be combined) to dial in different amounts of "spaciousness," and a mode switch to pick between the original DC-2 voicing and a brighter SDD-320 voicing.

## Controls
- **MODE SELECTOR [1]–[4] buttons**: Select the sound/intensity of the Dimension effect. Higher numbers produce a deeper effect. Pressing two buttons simultaneously combines their effects for additional variations.
- **S/SDD-320 switch**: Switches the overall sound character — "S (Standard)" reproduces the sound of the original DC-2; "SDD-320" reproduces a sparkling, brighter sound modeled on the SDD-320 Dimension D.

## Footswitches / Switches
- Single pedal switch: turns the effect on/off.
- CHECK indicator LED shows on/off state and doubles as a battery-check light.

## Notes for patch-building
- This is not a conventional chorus — there's no rate/depth control; the four mode buttons select fixed "amounts" of stereo spatial widening/thickening rather than a sweeping modulation. Think of it as a subtle stereo-widener/thickener more than a classic chorus warble.
- Stereo output only: OUTPUT A (MONO) outputs the effect sound in mono; OUTPUT B is for stereo use alongside A. The unit's audio output does not support mono-mixed stereo input, so for the optimal Dimension effect, feed it a stereo input and use it with stereo output.
- Good substitute for a chorus/MOD slot when the goal is "make this clean or lightly-driven tone feel bigger" without an obvious pitch-wobble character — useful on clean rhythm parts, ambient passages, or as a subtle widener under a lead rather than an obvious "chorus" effect.
- Buffered, standard Boss compact-pedal bypass; safe anywhere in the chain.
- Notable circuit lineage: Waza Craft-series analog recreation of a studio rack unit (Roland SDD-320) that shaped countless classic recordings' clean and chorus tones (e.g., Andy Summers/The Police-style shimmer).
