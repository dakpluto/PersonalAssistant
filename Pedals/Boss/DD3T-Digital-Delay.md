# Boss DD-3T Digital Delay

Compact digital delay pedal, a reissue of Boss's classic DD-3 (itself a descendant of the original DD-2/DD-3 lineage) with an added Tap Tempo function. One of the most widely used "utility" delays in guitar rigs — clean, reliable digital repeats rather than a modulated/lo-fi character.

## Controls
- **E.LEVEL (Effect Level)**: Sets the volume of the delayed (wet) signal, mixed with the dry signal
- **FEEDBACK**: Sets how many times the delayed signal repeats/decays (fully clockwise self-oscillates)
- **TIME**: Fine-adjusts the delay time within whichever range the MODE switch selects
- **MODE**: 4-position rotary selecting delay-time range — S (Short, ~12.5–50ms), M (Medium, ~50–200ms), L (Long, ~200–800ms), and HOLD (captures the input and loops it back, an infinite-repeat/looper-style effect, up to 800ms)

## Footswitches / Switches
- Single pedal switch: engages/bypasses the delay (buffered bypass)
- Hold the pedal switch ~2 seconds to toggle Tap Tempo entry mode (the DD-3T's addition over the original DD-3); once in tap mode, tapping the switch sets delay time, with selectable beat subdivisions
- External TEMPO jack accepts a separate footswitch for tapping tempo without touching the main switch

## Notes for patch-building
- Because it's buffered bypass, it's safe to place anywhere in a chain without loading/tone-suck concerns, and works well at the very end of a chain (after modulation, before reverb) or run through an amp's effects loop.
- DIRECT OUT jack sends a dry, unaffected copy of the input separately from the main OUTPUT — useful for splitting to a second amp or a DI for wet/dry rigs; not needed in a straightforward single-amp mono chain.
- The three-range MODE switch trades resolution for range: use S for tight slapback/doubling, M for typical rhythm delay, L for long ambient trails. Because MODE and TIME together only produce one delay time at once (no on-the-fly range switching via footswitch), a patch needing two very different delay times (e.g. short slapback + long ambient trail) needs two delay units/slots, not one DD-3T.
- Straightforward, all-digital repeats with no modulation or saturation — pairs well when the tonal color should come from elsewhere in the chain (verb, modulation, amp) and the delay itself should stay transparent.
