# Ibanez AD9 Analog Delay

A bucket-brigade (BBD) analog delay originally built by Ibanez/Maxon from 1982–1984 as part of the "9-series," replacing the earlier AD80. Prized for warm, treble-rolled-off repeats that degrade musically with each echo rather than staying pristine — the reference point a lot of later boutique analog delays chase. Ibanez currently keeps a reissue of the AD9 in production, built around new-manufacture BBD/clock chips (sourced from Shanghai Belling) rather than the original Panasonic parts, with an updated, lower-noise compander circuit versus the vintage unit.

## Controls
- **Delay Time**: Sets the delay time, roughly 10ms to 300ms — short by modern digital-delay standards (no tap tempo, presets, or delay-time switching; this is a short-to-medium slapback/echo range rather than a long ambient-delay pedal).
- **Repeat**: Sets feedback/number of repeats, from a single slapback up through longer decaying trails; designed to stay just short of runaway self-oscillation at full clockwise.
- **Delay Level**: Wet/dry mix — blends the delayed signal against the dry guitar signal.

## Jacks
- **Dual outputs**: A direct (dry) output and an effect (delayed) output, letting the dry and delayed signal be routed separately — e.g. to two amps — for a wider stereo-like spread than a single mono delay output gives.

## Notes for patch-building
- Circuit is BBD-based (MN3205 bucket-brigade chip with an MN3102 clock-driver chip, plus a JRC4558-based compander stage) — the source of its characteristic treble roll-off and "tape-like" degrading repeats, distinct from a clean digital delay.
- Typical placement: standard delay-block position, late in the chain after drive/amp-sim stages and (usually) before or alongside reverb — same convention as any time-based effect.
- Buffered bypass, not true bypass, per multiple sources (the coloration is described as mild/hard to notice in a chain).
- Short native delay-time ceiling (~300ms) means it's suited to slapback and mid-length rhythmic echo rather than long ambient trails — don't reach for it in a patch that needs long, sustained wash-of-delay tails.
- Mono in, dual (dry + effect) out; standard 9V battery or DC adapter power. Exact current draw for the modern reissue wasn't confirmed in research.
