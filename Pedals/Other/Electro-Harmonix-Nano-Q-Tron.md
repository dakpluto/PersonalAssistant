# Electro-Harmonix Nano Q-Tron

Compact envelope-controlled filter (auto-wah) descended from EHX's mid-90s Q-Tron, itself designed with input from Mike Beigel — creator of the original Musitronics Mu-Tron III, the effect's founding classic. The Q-Tron line built a strong funk/soul pedigree (Bootsy Collins, George Clinton) and the follow-up Q-Tron+ became John Mayer's go-to filter; the Nano packs that same dynamics-responsive filter circuit into EHX's small die-cast chassis. Known as one of the more aggressive, "phatter" envelope filters compared to a standard auto-wah, with genuinely deep Drive/Q interaction.

## Controls

- **Vol (Volume)**: Sets the pedal's output level.
- **Drive**: Sets the filter's sensitivity to playing dynamics — clockwise widens the sweep range and makes the filter react more dramatically (jump further in frequency) to picking attack; counterclockwise narrows the sweep for a subtler response.
- **Q**: Sets the resonance/bandwidth of the filter's peak — clockwise makes the filter more resonant and peaky, producing a more dramatic, vocal-like envelope effect; counterclockwise gives a smoother, less pronounced sweep.

## Switches

- **Mode (LP/BP/HP)**: Selects the filter type — **LP** (lowpass) emphasizes low frequencies for a fatter, rounder wah/synth-bass-like sweep; **BP** (bandpass) emphasizes midrange for the classic vocal "wah" quality; **HP** (highpass) emphasizes highs for a thinner, more clipped/nasal sweep.
- **Sweep direction (Up/Down)**: Sets whether the filter sweeps upward or downward in frequency in response to picking dynamics — reverses the fundamental contour of the envelope effect rather than just its intensity.

## Notes for patch-building

- Typical placement: filter/dynamics stage, generally early in the chain ahead of drive (classic funk placement, filtering the clean note attack) — though placing it after a drive stage for a filtered-distortion texture is also a legitimate, commonly used variant.
- True bypass; runs on a 9V battery or an optional 9VDC center-negative power supply (low current draw, ~10mA).
- Drive and Q are heavily interactive — Drive sets how far/hard the filter sweeps in response to attack, Q sets how narrow/resonant the sweep sounds at any given point, so a specific "quack" character needs both values documented together, not just one knob description.
- The BP mode plus moderate Drive/Q is the default "wah-like" envelope filter voice most players expect; LP and HP with the Up/Down switch open up more specialty textures (synth-bass-like sweeps, thin nasal effects) worth calling out explicitly when a patch uses them.
