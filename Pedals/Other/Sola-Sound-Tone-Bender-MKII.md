# Sola Sound Tone Bender Professional MKII

**Vintage/discontinued** — the original three-transistor germanium fuzz introduced by Sola Sound (London) in 1966, the pedal that gave the Tone Bender family its name and reputation. Made famous through the mid-to-late-60s British rock scene — most notably Jimmy Page's use on Led Zeppelin's debut album, and earlier use by Jeff Beck — its aggressive, harmonically dense, high-sustain fuzz sits between a Fuzz Face's smoothness and a Big Muff's saturation. Included here as reference only; original period units are rare and expensive collector pieces. Sola Sound (via the Macari family business) and boutique builder D*A*M (Differential Audio Manifestationz) currently produce small-batch officially-licensed Tone Bender MKII reissues, and numerous other boutique clones exist (Analog Man, JMI, Fuzz Central, etc.) — this file documents the original circuit's controls, not any specific reissue's added features.

## Controls
- **Level** (Volume): Output level of the pedal
- **Attack**: Fuzz intensity/gain — the MKII's defining control, driving the three-germanium-transistor gain stage into heavy saturation
- **Tone**: Overall tonal balance/brightness

## Notes for patch-building
- Circuit is a three-transistor germanium design (typically NKT275 or similar PNP germanium transistors, later clones commonly substitute AC128 or Tesla 3AX31-series) — one extra gain stage ahead of the earlier two-transistor Tone Bender MKI, giving the MKII a thicker, more sustained, more harmonically complex fuzz than its predecessor.
- Like most vintage germanium fuzzes, it's sensitive to the input impedance in front of it — wants to see the guitar's pickups directly (first in the chain, before any buffered pedal or wah) to avoid the fuzz sounding thin, choked, or "farty."
- Not true bypass in the modern sense — original units used simple mechanical footswitching typical of mid-60s pedal design, without a dedicated bypass buffer; germanium fuzzes of this era are also temperature-sensitive, with tone/gain shifting somewhat as the transistors warm up.
- Cleans up via the guitar's volume knob more dramatically than a Big Muff, closer to a Fuzz Face in that regard, though the extra gain stage keeps more saturation on tap even at lower guitar-volume settings.
- Mono in/out, no onboard 9V jack on original units (battery-only, as was standard for the era) — modern reissues typically add a DC jack.
- Treat this as reference-only for tonal character (e.g. a "Tone Bender-y" British fuzz tone) rather than a pick for an actual current build — for a buildable current option, a D*A*M or other licensed Sola Sound reissue would be the closer real-world equivalent.
