# Electro-Harmonix Small Stone Nano

Compact reissue of EHX's 1974 four-stage OTA phaser, arguably the second most famous phase shifter ever built after the MXR Phase 90. Known for a smooth, sweet, slightly darker phase sweep than the Phase 90's more vocal character, and for the "Issue J" 1977 circuit revision widely regarded as the best-sounding version — the modern Nano reissue is built around that circuit in a small die-cast enclosure. Heard on countless 70s-through-modern recordings across genres.

## Controls

- **Rate**: Sets the speed of the phase-shift sweep. Clockwise speeds it up, from slow, lush pulsing to fast, warbling sweeps.

## Switches

- **Color**: Adds feedback/resonance into the phaser circuit. Down position gives the full, robust classic Small Stone sweep; up position pushes into a more pronounced, hollowed-out phase shift that carves more aggressively into the frequency spectrum (a more intense, "swooshier" voice than the Color-off setting).

## Notes for patch-building

- Typical placement: modulation stage, alongside chorus/flanger-type effects, generally after drive/amp stages and before delay/reverb — standard phaser placement, though phasers are also sometimes placed pre-drive for a different, more subdued interaction (either is a legitimate choice depending on desired texture).
- Bypass note: EHX documents the Nano Small Stone as using "true bypass" in marketing copy, but at least one manufacturer/support source describes its actual bypass implementation as "pseudo true bypass" (signal routed directly input-to-output in bypass, without a full mechanical relay/3PDT switch) — flag this as an unresolved discrepancy between sources rather than asserting one or the other definitively.
- Only two controls (Rate + Color switch) means very few degrees of freedom versus richer phasers — a patch using this pedal should specify Rate as a clock position/percentage and Color on/off rather than assuming finer shaping is available.
- Standard 9V DC power (adapter sold separately) or 9V battery; status LED lights when the effect is engaged, off when bypassed.
- The full-size (non-Nano) Small Stone shares the identical two-control circuit in a larger enclosure — functionally interchangeable for patch-building purposes; the Nano is simply the smaller current-production chassis.
