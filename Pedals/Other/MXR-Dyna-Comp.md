# MXR Dyna Comp (M102)

Classic optical-style analog compressor, in continuous production since the mid-1970s and still a Nashville studio-session standard for its smooth, squashy sustain. Two-knob simplicity — Output and Sensitivity are the whole story — and its "quack"/pumping character at high compression settings has become as much a signature sound as a utility tool.

## Controls
- **Output**: Overall output volume of the effect. Clockwise for more; used to make up for the level loss compression introduces.
- **Sensitivity**: Sets the compression ratio/amount. Clockwise increases sensitivity (more compression, more sustain, more audible "squash" and pumping); counterclockwise gives lighter, more transparent compression.

## Notes for patch-building
- Typical placement: compression stage, early in the chain ahead of drive/distortion (classic position — smooths pick-attack dynamics before they hit gain stages), though some players run it after drive for a different, more "glued" effect; either is a legitimate patch choice worth documenting explicitly.
- True hardwire bypass, mono in/out, standard 9V DC (2.1mm center-negative via Dunlop ECB003 or MXR Brick supply, or 9V battery), 3.3mA draw.
- Fixed internal Attack (5ms) and Release (1s) times — no separate attack/release controls, so all dynamic shaping comes from the two knobs; at high Sensitivity settings expect audible sustain "swell" and some pumping/breathing rather than clean, invisible leveling.
- Max compression spec'd at 36dB — this can get aggressive fast; a patch aiming for subtle sustain-only compression should keep Sensitivity well below noon.
