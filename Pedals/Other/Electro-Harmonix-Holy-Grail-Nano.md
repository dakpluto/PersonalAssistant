# Electro-Harmonix Holy Grail Nano

Compact digital reverb descended from EHX's original Holy Grail — one of the first affordable, mass-market digital reverb pedals, built around a dedicated reverb chip Mike Matthews sourced in the early 2000s that made studio-style reverb algorithms viable in a stompbox for the first time. The Nano repackages the classic single-knob, three-mode Holy Grail circuit into a small die-cast enclosure. This is the current, minimalist Holy Grail model; EHX also currently sells the more full-featured Holy Grail Max (Blend/Decay knobs, Spring/Hall/Plate/Reverse algorithm selector) and Holy Grail Neo (Spring/Hall/Plate) for players who want more control than the Nano's single knob — this write-up covers the Nano specifically.

## Controls

- **Reverb**: Acts as a dry/wet blend rather than a pure decay-length control. From minimum to about the 12 o'clock position it adds a subtle amount of reverb to the dry signal; from 12 o'clock to maximum the wet reverb signal increases dramatically, quickly dominating the mix as it approaches full clockwise.

## Switches

- **Mode select (3-way slide switch)**: Chooses the reverb algorithm — **Spring** (classic spring-tank emulation, boingy/twangy character), **Hall** (smooth, larger digital hall reverb voiced for guitar), **Flerb** (EHX's flanged spatial reverb — combines flanging with the reverb tail for a swirling, ethereal texture unlike a standard reverb type).

## Notes for patch-building

- Typical placement: end of chain, after drive/modulation/delay — standard reverb placement.
- True bypass; powered by a 9.6VDC-200mA center-negative adapter (included) — no battery option confirmed, unlike many other EHX nano pedals.
- Because the single Reverb knob is a blend control rather than separate decay/mix controls, dialing in a specific wet level is coarse compared to pedals with independent decay and mix — for a patch needing a precise wash amount, document the exact knob position (e.g. "just past 12 o'clock") rather than a vague "medium reverb."
- Flerb is a genuinely different effect from Spring/Hall (adds flanging into the reverb tail) — worth flagging explicitly in a patch write-up as a specialty texture choice, not just "reverb mode 3."
- If a patch wants independent control over decay time and dry/wet mix, or a Reverse algorithm, the Holy Grail Max is the current EHX sibling pedal with that expanded control set — the Nano documented here trades that flexibility for simplicity/size.
