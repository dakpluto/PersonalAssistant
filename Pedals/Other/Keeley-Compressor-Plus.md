# Keeley Compressor Plus

Studio-style compressor/sustainer pedal from Keeley Electronics, built on the long-running Keeley compressor circuit lineage that put the company on the map. Marketed as "the most popular boutique compressor in the world" — Keeley cites over 132,000 units built as of February 2024, and it was Reverb.com's best-selling pedal overall in 2021. Reputation is for smooth, studio-quality compression with an unusually musical Blend control for parallel compression, rather than the choppier/more obviously "squashed" character of simpler compressor pedals.

## Controls
- **Sustain**: Sets the amount of compression/expansion applied to the input signal — clockwise increases compression, giving longer sustain and more evened-out dynamics.
- **Attack**: Controls how quickly the compressor responds to incoming signal (response speed).
- **Level**: Makeup-gain control; restores (or boosts past) unity gain, since compression inherently reduces output volume.
- **Blend**: Mixes the compressed signal with the original dry signal — clockwise for more compressed signal, counterclockwise for more dry. Enables parallel compression that preserves pick attack and transient dynamics rather than compressing the entire signal uniformly.
- **Tone**: Custom emphasis circuit that restores/brightens the treble content commonly lost during compression.

## Switches
- **Release (Single Coil / Humbucker)**: Two-position toggle that changes the compressor's release voicing for the pickup type in use — Humbucker mode releases more quickly to avoid a "dull" sound from humbuckers' higher average output energy; Single Coil mode uses a slower release suited to lower-output pickups.

## Notes for patch-building
- Typical placement: compression stage, early in the chain (ahead of drive/amp) — standard position for a compressor feeding a clean or breaking-up front end, evening out pick dynamics before the drive/amp stages shape the tone.
- True bypass; 9VDC center-negative power, low ~10mA current draw.
- The Blend knob is the main patch-relevant differentiator versus a simpler compressor — a patch wanting transparent sustain with preserved attack should favor a lower Blend (more dry signal mixed back in) rather than max compression.
- A recommended neutral starting point (per Keeley's own materials) is all four knobs — Sustain, Level, Blend, Tone — at 12 o'clock, with the Release switch set to match the guitar's pickup type.
