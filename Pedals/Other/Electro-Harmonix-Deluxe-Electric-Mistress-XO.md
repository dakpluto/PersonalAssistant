# Electro-Harmonix Deluxe Electric Mistress XO

Analog BBD flanger descended directly from EHX's original 1976 Electric Mistress, one of the first flanger pedals ever built and a defining voice of the effect — David Gilmour used it extensively on Pink Floyd's *The Wall*, and Andy Summers built much of The Police's guitar sound around it. The Deluxe version added the Filter Matrix mode; the XO repackages that same analog Deluxe circuit into a compact die-cast enclosure with true bypass and an LED. Status note: ehx.com currently lists the "Deluxe Electric Mistress" product page as discontinued (2023), and the same analog circuit has since been relaunched under EHX's Andy Summers signature "Walking on the Moon" flanger with an identical Rate/Range/Color/Filter Matrix control set — but the "Deluxe Electric Mistress XO" (MISTRESS XO) SKU still appeared as an orderable, non-discontinued listing on shop.ehx.com and at multiple dealers as of this research. Treat the XO as the current small-format analog Electric Mistress unless Michael confirms it's been fully phased out in favor of the Andy Summers model.

## Controls

- **Rate**: Sets the speed of the flanger's LFO sweep. Turning clockwise speeds it up; at higher settings the modulation moves fast enough to sound vibrato-like rather than a slow sweep.
- **Range**: Sets the lower limit of the flanger sweep. Fully counterclockwise keeps the sweep narrow (low limit close to the high limit); turning clockwise extends the sweep further down into the bass register, deepening the effect.
- **Color**: Sets the intensity/depth of the flanging effect — clockwise increases the pronounced "whoosh" that's the flanger's signature comb-filter sound.

## Switches

- **Filter Matrix**: Disconnects the LFO from the flanger circuit, freezing the sweep at a fixed point rather than continuously modulating. With the LFO frozen, Range sets the fixed filter position and Color sets filtering depth, producing static chime-like/metallic comb-filtering rather than a moving flange — a distinct sound from the normal sweeping mode, not just "flanger off."

## Jacks

- **Input**: 1/4" instrument input, 600k input impedance.
- **Flanged Out**: 1/4" main effect output.
- **Dry Out**: 1/4" output wired directly to the input, giving an unaffected parallel signal to split to a second amp or effects chain.
- **Power**: 9V DC center-negative, included 9.6VDC-200mA adapter or 9V battery.

## Notes for patch-building

- Typical placement: modulation stage, after drive/amp-sim stages, alongside/ahead of other MOD-type effects and before delay/reverb — standard flanger placement.
- True bypass, mono in with a parallel dry-out tap; standard center-negative 9V power (adapter included, but confirm current draw against the target power supply's mA budget before adding to a shared board).
- The Filter Matrix mode is functionally a second effect living in the same pedal (static comb-filter/chime voice vs. sweeping flange) — worth calling out explicitly in a patch write-up rather than lumping it in as "flanger with the switch flipped," since it changes the fundamental character of the sound.
- Rate/Range/Color interact: Range sets how deep the sweep goes and Color sets how pronounced it sounds, so a specific classic "whoosh" tone needs all three documented together rather than a single "high/low" descriptor.
- Current-production status is genuinely ambiguous per ehx.com vs. dealer listings as of this research (see intro paragraph) — verify against ehx.com's live catalog before assuming this exact SKU is still orderable new; the Andy Summers "Walking on the Moon" flanger uses the same core Rate/Range/Color/Filter Matrix circuit if the XO turns out to be fully gone.
