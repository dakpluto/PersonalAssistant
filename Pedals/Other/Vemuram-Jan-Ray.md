# Vemuram Jan Ray

Flagship low/medium-gain overdrive from Vemuram, the Tokyo-based boutique brand founded in 2008 (Masaki Uemura with engineer Mr. Tezuka) known for hand-built, solid-brass-enclosure pedals. The Jan Ray is voiced after 1960s Blackface Fender amp breakup — designed to stay out of the way of the guitar and amp's own character rather than impose a drive-pedal "flavor" on top of it — and has built a strong reputation among session and touring professionals (particularly in Nashville) as one of the most transparent, amp-like overdrives available.

## Controls
- **Level**: Output volume. Commonly run above unity to push a clean amp into breakup, or set closer to unity to add clarity/sustain while staying transparent.
- **Gain**: Overdrive intensity. Low settings (roughly 2-3 on the dial per user-reported settings) sit at edge-of-breakup; higher settings (7-8+) move into cranked, saturated lead drive.
- **Bass**: Low-end response — lower settings tighten the tone, higher settings add warmth/fullness.
- **Treble**: High-end presence — higher settings add bite and clarity for leads, lower settings smooth the top end.
- **Internal saturation trim pot** (rear panel, accessed with a small screwdriver, not a front-panel control): adjusts overall saturation character — turned down for more transparency, turned up to tame the high end and produce a fatter, more compressed tone. A second internal bass trim pot has also been reported by users for balancing low end across different amp/pickup pairings, though Vemuram's own documentation of exact trim-pot behavior could not be confirmed from official sources.

## Notes for patch-building
- Typical placement: transparent drive/boost stage, early in the gain chain — works well as an always-on "amp enhancer" ahead of a higher-gain DST/AMP stage, or alone for edge-of-breakup tones.
- Especially responsive to guitar volume-knob dynamics and pickup choice — pairs particularly well with single-coils for clarity/chime; a patch using this pedal (or emulating its behavior in the GP-5's own DST/PRE modules) should lean toward touch-sensitive, low-to-moderate gain settings rather than fixed high saturation.
- Works well stacked ahead of another drive for extended gain range, consistent with its "transparent" design goal.
- Bypass type (true vs. buffered) could not be confirmed from manufacturer or secondary sources — treat as unknown/unverified rather than assuming true bypass.
- Front-panel controls are reportedly unlabeled on the pedal itself (Level/Gain/Bass/Treble order confirmed via retailer and review documentation, not a manufacturer-printed legend) — worth double-checking against the physical unit if Michael owns one before wiring settings into a patch write-up.
