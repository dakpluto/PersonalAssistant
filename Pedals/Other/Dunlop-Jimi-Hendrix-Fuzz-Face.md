# Dunlop Jimi Hendrix Fuzz Face Distortion (JHF1)

Signature-edition reproduction of the 1969-70 Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face Jimi Hendrix used on recordings like Electric Ladyland — round enclosure, two knobs, one of the most direct lines to classic silicon fuzz tone in current production. Dunlop describes it as "meticulously faithful" to the original unit's spec.

## Controls
- **Volume**: Overall effect output volume. Clockwise for more.
- **Fuzz**: Amount of fuzz/gain. Clockwise for more.

## Notes for patch-building
- Silicon-transistor circuit (built around the BC108 silicon transistor) — the current-production JHF1 is silicon only, not a germanium/silicon-switchable model. For a germanium/silicon-switchable Hendrix-spec Fuzz Face, Dunlop separately sells the Authentic Hendrix '68 Shrine Series Fuzz Face (JHMS1, mini enclosure), which adds a germanium/silicon toggle plus a buffer/true-bypass switch — a different current-production model from this one.
- Typical placement: fuzz stage, early in the drive order like most fuzzes — wants the guitar's pickups fairly directly, ahead of buffers, wah, and other pedals with input-impedance-sensitive interactions (classic Fuzz Face circuits are known to react poorly when placed after a wah or buffered pedal).
- True hardwire bypass, mono in/out, powered only by a single internal 9V battery (no adapter jack documented in the manufacturer manual) — worth flagging for a fixed pedalboard build where always-on DC power is assumed.
- Only two controls (no separate tone knob) — like other classic Fuzz Face circuits, Volume and Fuzz interact with the guitar's own volume knob and pickup type more than most modern fuzzes; tonal shaping beyond the fuzz character itself has to come from the guitar or a downstream EQ/amp stage.
