# Maestro Fuzz-Tone FZ-M

Current-production fuzz from Gibson's revived Maestro brand (relaunched 2022), directly referencing the original Maestro FZ-1 — the first commercially available fuzzbox, released by Gibson in 1962 and designed by Nashville engineers Glenn Snoddy and Revis V. Hobbs after they replicated a fuzzy tone caused by a faulty mixing console. The FZ-1's popularity exploded after Keith Richards used one on the Rolling Stones' "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction" in 1965. The FZ-M packages both that vintage voice and a thicker modern fuzz circuit in one pedal.

## Controls
- **Attack**: Dials in the fuzz/gain level, from mild breakup to heavy saturation.
- **Tone**: Shapes timbre from bright and raspy to warm and wooly.
- **Level**: Output volume; can go well beyond unity gain.

## Switches
- **Mode (Classic/Modern)**: Toggles between an FZ-1-inspired vintage fuzz voice and a thicker, more modern fuzz voice — effectively two fuzz circuits in one enclosure.

## Notes for patch-building
- Typical placement: fuzz stage, early in the drive order like most fuzzes (wants a fairly direct feed from the guitar's pickups).
- True bypass footswitch; the bypass LEDs are built into the "bugles" of the Maestro logo on the enclosure.
- Power: 9V battery or 9VDC center-negative power supply via a 5.5x2.1mm plug.
- The Mode switch is the key patch-defining decision here — pick Classic for a period-accurate '60s fuzz character or Modern for a thicker/heavier voice; document which one a patch uses since it's a fundamentally different circuit, not just a tone tweak.
- All-analog circuit on glass-epoxy boards; this is the current official Gibson/Maestro reissue, not a vintage original, so it's a safe pick for "grab a real one today" scenarios.
