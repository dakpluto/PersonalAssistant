# Horizon Devices Precision Drive

High-gain metal overdrive built by Jim Dunlop/MXR for Horizon Devices, the gear company founded by Misha Mansoor of Periphery (with Mehtab Bhogal and Brian Gilmanov). Released in 2016 after an extensive development process (reportedly 400+ hours, with the built-in noise gate redesigned seven times), it's purpose-built for tight, articulate, modern metal tones — designed to tighten and focus an amp's low end for palm-muted riffing and extended-range/down-tuned guitars rather than function as a general-purpose drive.

## Controls
- **Gain**: Overdrive/saturation amount. Design intent is to start near zero and dial up gradually — official guidance suggests starting clean and increasing until the desired thickness/saturation is reached, rather than treating it like a fixed-character gain knob.
- **Volume**: Output level; official guidance notes the pedal runs hot relative to a typical Tube Screamer-style pedal (roughly "6 on the Precision Drive is like 10 on a Tube Screamer"), so start conservatively.
- **Bright**: Adds presence/high-end sizzle, intended to help darker or muddier amps cut through. Official guidance warns it can get harsh in its upper range when paired with low/medium-gain tones, so back it off for those settings.
- **Gate**: Built-in noise gate threshold — counterclockwise gives a loose, subtle gate; clockwise gives a fast, hard, more aggressive gate that also tightens bass response. Noon is a reasonable starting middle ground.

## Switches
- **Attack (6-position rotary switch)**: Not a continuous knob — a stepped selector that progressively reshapes low-end tightness and pick response. Counterclockwise positions favor an older, thicker character with more lower-mid punch; clockwise positions get tighter, more defined, and pickier/more modern, suited to fast palm-muted playing.

## Notes for patch-building
- Typical placement: gain stage, mid-to-late in the drive order — designed to sit in front of an amp (tube or digital/modeler) as a tightening/saturation boost specifically for high-gain metal rhythm and lead tones, not as a transparent low-gain overdrive.
- True bypass, mono in/out; powered by a 9VDC center-negative supply (9V battery also supported); current draw approximately 16mA.
- The Attack switch and Gate control are the two settings most specific to this pedal's metal-focused design — a patch write-up should call out both explicitly rather than lumping them in as generic "tone" settings, since they materially change low-end tightness and note articulation together with Gain.
- Distinct from Horizon Devices' later single-knob "Nano Attack" pedal, which isolates just the Attack control in its own enclosure — not to be confused with the Precision Drive's onboard Attack switch.
