# Catalinbread Karma Suture

Catalinbread's take on the ultra-rare early-1970s Interfax Harmonic Percolator circuit — not a direct clone, but a reworking that keeps the Percolator's asymmetrical, even-order-harmonic-heavy clipping while adding new interactive controls. It shipped in two versions: **Karma Suture (Ge)**, pairing an NOS PNP germanium transistor with a silicon NPN transistor (marketed by Catalinbread as an overdrive/distortion pedal), and **Karma Suture (Si)**, an all-silicon-transistor version (marketed as a "silicon harmonic fuzz"); both share the same four-knob layout and circuit topology, differing mainly in transistor type, EQ/bias component values, and resulting voicing. Catalinbread has since discontinued regular production of the Karma Suture (a limited gold-finish Ge reissue was later released in a run of 150 units), so treat it as a legacy/boutique reference rather than a current catalog item.

## Controls
- **Input**: Sets how much guitar signal reaches the harmonic-generator circuit — low settings clean things up into a harmonically-rich boost, full up drives the circuit hard for fuzz-like blasts.
- **Output**: Overall output level control.
- **Density**: An input-capacitor blend control (per Catalinbread's own circuit notes) that reshapes the drive character — ranges from a thick, amp-on-the-verge-of-exploding fuzz tone at one extreme to a tighter, harmonically rich rock overdrive at the other. Renamed from the original Harmonic Percolator's "Harmonics"/"Balance" knobs (Input/Output/Density/Diodes is Catalinbread's own relabeling of that circuit's controls).
- **Diodes**: Blends in the clipping diode stage — described by Catalinbread as functioning like a reverse volume control for clipping: at minimum, diode influence is limited (via a ~50k resistance in the circuit); turning it up progressively increases clipping intensity and compression, up to full diode clipping.

## Notes for patch-building
- Typical placement: drive stage, early-to-mid in the chain like a conventional overdrive/fuzz — the Ge version leans overdrive/amp-like breakup, the Si version leans harder into fuzz/boost territory; pick the version to reference based on which character the patch wants.
- The even-order-harmonic-heavy clipping is specifically praised (per manufacturer and reviewers) for retaining note clarity on complex chords even at extreme settings — a reasonable pick when a patch wants a thick/fuzzy drive that doesn't turn to mud on chords.
- Density and Diodes interact with Input/Output rather than behaving as independent tone controls, so a patch write-up should record the exact 4-knob setting rather than a qualitative "high gain" description.
- Bypass type (true bypass vs. buffered) was not confirmed by manufacturer or retailer sources found in this research — do not assume true bypass; note it as unconfirmed if it matters for a specific board's signal-chain planning.
- Power: 9V DC (battery or 9-18V DC external supply, center-negative), with an unusually low quoted current draw of roughly 1mA on the Si version — treat that figure as version-specific and unconfirmed for the Ge version.
