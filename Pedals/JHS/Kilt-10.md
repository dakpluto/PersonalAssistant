# JHS Kilt 10

Two-in-one dirt box/boost built with UK-based guitarist Stu G (Delirious?), spanning overdrive through gated fuzz via a pair of clipping toggles. "10" marks the current polished-aluminum anniversary redesign of the original Kilt, with wider-range Gain and Tone sweeps than the earlier version and JHS's Red Remote jack for remote-switching the gain stage on the fly.

## Controls
- **Volume**: Output level. Left is less, right is more — the redesign added headroom so unity is reachable well before noon.
- **Gain**: Amount of drive/distortion. Left is less, right is more; the first 25% of the sweep now yields usable transparent-overdrive tones rather than jumping straight to grind.
- **Tone**: Brightness. Left is darker, right is brighter.

## Footswitches / Switches
- **G1 toggle** and **G2 toggle**: Work together to select clipping resistance — four combinations spanning overdrive to fuzz:
  - Both down: lowest-gain setting
  - G1 up, G2 down: more overdrive
  - G1 down, G2 up: more fuzz
  - Both up: heaviest, gated fuzz voicing
- **Low-Cut toggle**: Down = flat EQ; up = cut mode, reduces bass for a leaner tone that cuts through a mix.
- **Red Remote jack**: Accepts JHS's Red Remote accessory to flip the G1/G2 gain toggle remotely (e.g., via an external footswitch) for instant overdrive-to-distortion or distortion-to-fuzz jumps mid-song.
- Single main footswitch, true bypass.

## Notes for patch-building
- Genuinely spans two gain categories in one box — think of the G1/G2 combinations as four internal "voices" (clean-ish OD, hotter OD, fuzz, gated fuzz) rather than a single fixed distortion character; pick the combination that matches the target tone since the GP-5 patch can't reproduce Red Remote's live toggle mid-patch.
- Typical placement: drive section of the chain, ahead of the amp; the Low-Cut toggle is useful when stacking into an already-thick amp voicing or when the fuzz settings get muddy.
- True bypass, mono in/out, standard 9V DC center-negative (max 9V, do not exceed), 64mA draw.
