# JHS Muffuletta

Six-in-one Big Muff Pi-style fuzz — a rotary Mode selector switches between component values recreating six historical Muff circuit eras, spanning the earliest Triangle version through JHS's own take, in one enclosure.

## Controls
- **Volume**: Overall output level.
- **Fuzz**: Amount of gain/fuzz for the selected Mode.
- **Tone**: Tone-shaping for the selected Mode (exact voicing varies per circuit era, as on the originals).

## Footswitches / Switches
- **Mode** (6-position rotary selector): Selects between six Big Muff-lineage circuits:
  1. **The Triangle (1969–1970, V1)** — earliest version; enhanced low-end response and note articulation
  2. **'73 Rams Head (1973–1977, V2)** — scooped midrange, lower gain, darker overall
  3. **The Pi (1977–1978, V3)** — more aggressive than the Rams Head
  4. **The Civil War (1991–1993, V7)** — brighter overall, more midrange presence
  5. **The Russian (1999–2009, V8)** — lower clarity and low end, smoother/garage-style tone
  6. **JHS Mode (2015)** — JHS's original take: more powerful, less compressed, with a more pronounced/haunting midrange
- Single footswitch.

## Notes for patch-building
- Note: sold as a 6-mode selector (not 5) despite sometimes being described as "5-in-1" in older marketing/reviews — document the Mode chosen since it's a fixed rotary setting per patch, not footswitch-toggleable.
- Scooped-mid fuzz character varies significantly by mode — Triangle/Rams Head/Pi lean into the classic thick, wall-of-fuzz Muff sound with varying gain/scoop; Civil War is brighter and more cutting; Russian is smoother/lower-fi; JHS Mode is the most modern/aggressive.
- Typical placement: fuzz stage of the chain — Big Muff-style circuits are famously picky about pedal order (often placed before a wah or before/after other gain stages depending on desired stacking); if stacking with another drive, expect interaction and test both orders.
- Mono in/out, standard 9V DC negative, 4mA draw.
