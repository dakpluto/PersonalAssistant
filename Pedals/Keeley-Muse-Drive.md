# Keeley Muse Drive (Mk3 Driver — Andy Timmons Signature Overdrive)

Andy Timmons signature overdrive; Keeley has since renamed this pedal from "Muse Driver" to "Mk3 Driver" (same circuit — germanium diodes, JFET gain stage, and op-amp clipping stack, built from a three-control blues-driver-style circuit with two voicing switches). Covers clean boost through overdrive to a soft, germanium-flavored fuzz.

## Controls
- **Level**: Output volume.
- **Tone**: Frequency shaping; interacts with the Tone switch position below.
- **Drive**: Gain/overdrive amount.

## Footswitches / Switches
- **Drive switch (AT / RK)**: Selects the clipping character. AT (up) = asymmetrical LED + silicon diode clipping, Andy's "Super AT Mod" voice. RK (down) = germanium diode clipping, a softer, smoother, more vintage-fuzz-like feel.
- **Tone switch (AT / RK)**: Selects the low-end voicing the Tone knob works with. AT (up) = Andy's preferred low-end response. RK (down) = "Phat Boost" low-end voicing, aimed at pushing vintage/single-coil pickups harder.
- **Footswitch**: Standard engage/bypass, with silent (click-free) switching. Hold for 2 seconds to toggle the pedal's bypass mode itself between true bypass (LED blinks twice) and buffered bypass (LED blinks three times).

## Notes for patch-building
- Front-end drive/boost stage — same functional slot as the GP-5's DST or a boost pedal ahead of AMP. The AT/RK switch pair means this one pedal can cover two quite different drive voicings (LED-clipped AT mod vs. softer germanium RK mode), useful when a song needs a drive character the GP-5's own DST models don't quite nail.
- Because bypass mode (true vs. buffered) is user-selectable, note which mode a patch assumes if the rest of the board is buffer-sensitive (e.g., long true-bypass cable runs elsewhere).
- Runs on 9V or 18V DC — running at 18V (if the board's supply allows it) increases headroom/clean threshold, worth flagging for patches wanting extra dynamic range before clipping kicks in.
