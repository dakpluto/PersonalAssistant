# J. Rockett Audio Designs Airchild Six Sixty (Airchild 660)

Studio-style compressor pedal modeled on the sound of the legendary Fairchild 660 tube limiter. VCA-based circuit design aimed at reproducing that unit's harmonic richness and "chime," with a blendable/tilt-EQ control set rather than a simple attack/release compressor layout.

## Controls
- **Threshold**: Amount of compression, from minimal to maximum "crush."
- **Output**: Output level — also interacts with Threshold to determine how hard the pedal pushes the front end of an amp (or, in a GP-5 signal chain, the following module).
- **Tone**: Tilt-EQ. Clockwise boosts highs and cuts lows; counterclockwise boosts lows and cuts highs.
- **Blend**: Mixes dry (uncompressed) signal with the compressed signal, for parallel-compression-style control rather than pure series compression.

## Notes for patch-building
- Functions as an always-on-style studio compressor rather than a subtle "invisible" compressor — designed to add harmonic richness and even out transients while still letting some character/growl through, so it can double as a light front-end coloration/boost stage as well as dynamics control.
- Typical placement is early in the chain, before drive/distortion stages, the same general role a compressor pedal plays ahead of the GP-5's DST/AMP — the Blend control makes it flexible for either a squashed, fully-compressed setting or a lighter parallel-compression touch.
- Single footswitch with LED status indicator; 9V DC power, ~20mA draw. Bypass type (true vs. buffered) was not confirmed in available manufacturer/retailer documentation — verify directly with J. Rockett if this matters for a specific build.
- Despite the "tube limiter" heritage it emulates, the pedal itself is solid-state (VCA-based), not a real tube circuit.
