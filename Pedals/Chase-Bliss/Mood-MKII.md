# Chase Bliss MOOD MKII

Two-channel granular micro-looper/delay/reverb. The Loop channel (designed with Drolo FX) is an always-listening micro-looper with granular playback modes; the Wet channel (designed with Old Blood Noise Endeavors) is a spatial effects channel offering reverb, delay, or a granular "slip" pitch/time effect. The two channels run in parallel and blend together.

## Controls
- **Clock**: Sets the sample rate for the whole pedal, from clean/modern down to lofi/degraded — stepped in musical intervals by default, with a "smooth" continuous sweep available via dip switch
- **Time**: Function changes with Wet-channel mode — Reverb: decay/size; Delay: delay time; Slip: refresh rate
- **Modify (Wet channel)**: Function changes with mode — Reverb: smear/diffusion; Delay: feedback; Slip: playback speed and direction
- **Length (Loop channel)**: Sets the micro-loop's length
- **Modify (Loop channel)**: Shapes/manipulates the captured loop; behavior depends on the Loop channel's mode
- **Mix/Ramp**: Overall balance between dry input and MOOD's combined wet output (controls both channels together); can also ramp/ease between settings
- 3-way mode switch (Wet channel): selects Reverb / Delay / Slip
- 3-way mode switch (Loop channel): selects among the Loop channel's granular playback modes (e.g. stretch/tape/envelope-style behaviors)
- 3-way routing switch: sets how the Loop and Wet channels are routed relative to each other (parallel/series-style options)

## Footswitches / Switches
- Left footswitch: bypass (switchable true or buffered bypass)
- Right footswitch: engages/controls the always-on Loop channel's playback (the micro-looper is continuously listening; stepping on this footswitch triggers it to start playing back captured audio)
- Preset / Live / Preset 3-way switch: selects operating mode for preset recall vs. live-tweak behavior
- Row of DIP switches: routing and behavior options, including a ping-pong stereo mode, "classic" mode (restores MKI-era behavior), and the smooth-Clock option

## Notes for patch-building
- Functions as a combined delay/reverb/looper block — on a GP-5-based board it's a natural substitute or supplement for the DLY/RVB slots, or an ambient layer added after the GP-5 entirely, rather than a drive or amp-stage tool.
- The granular Loop channel gives it a distinct "instant ambient pad from whatever you just played" role that a conventional delay/reverb can't do — useful for building swells or drones under a lead line.
- Stereo TRS I/O (mono also supported); switchable true/buffered bypass.
- All parameters are MIDI-controllable and both channels can sync to MIDI clock — the MKII adds a note/transposition mode that turns the pedal into a granular synth voice via MIDI keyboard, which is companion-control depth well beyond what the six physical knobs alone expose.
