# Keeley Blues Disorder

Overdrive/distortion pedal combining two classic circuits — a Marshall Bluesbreaker-style (BB) drive and a Fulltone OCD-style (OC) drive — into one three-knob box, with switches that let you mix and match halves of each circuit for four distinct voicings.

## Controls
- **Drive**: Overdrive/distortion gain amount.
- **Tone**: High-frequency roll-off/shaping.
- **Level**: Output volume.

## Switches
- **Tone Switch (BB/OC)**: Selects which circuit's tone stage is in use. BB position is a simple, transparent high-frequency roll-off with no mid-boost. OC position adds an active midrange boost with treble bleed from the Tone knob.
- **Drive Clipping Switch (BB/OC)**: Selects the diode clipping style. BB position is soft clipping (smoother, tube-amp-like breakup). OC position is hard clipping (edgier, more distinctly distorted).
- The two switches combine independently, giving four tonal combinations: both up = smooth soft-clipping "Bluesy Breaker"; both down = fuller, hard-clipping "OCD-style" distortion; and two hybrid combinations mixing one circuit's clipping with the other's tone stage.

## Footswitches / Switches
- Single footswitch: engages/bypasses the pedal. Hold for two seconds to toggle between true bypass and buffered bypass modes.

## Notes for patch-building
- Functions as a front-end drive/overdrive stage — same role as the GP-5's own DST module, or as a boost/second gain stage feeding into DST+AMP if used alongside the GP-5's onboard drive.
- The BB-tone-stack + soft-clipping combo is the lowest-gain, most transparent setting (good rhythm push into a clean or edge-of-breakup amp); the OC-tone-stack + hard-clipping combo is the highest-gain, most colored setting (good standalone distortion or lead voicing).
- Because the two switches are independent, a single physical pedal setting only gives you one of the four voicings at a time — if a patch needs two distinct drive characters, that still requires two module slots or two separate pedals, same constraint as the GP-5's own DST.
- Switchable true/buffered bypass is a real quirk worth knowing when chaining with other true-bypass pedals earlier or later in a signal path.
